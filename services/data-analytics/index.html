THE PURPOSE

Better decisions start
with a reliable question.

A dashboard is not the starting point. First, define the decision, identify the relevant records, and check how the numbers are produced. Then build a report that a person can use without needing to decode it.

A GOOD STARTING POINT WHEN

  • You spend too long reconciling spreadsheets before a meeting.
  • Different reports disagree about the same metric.
  • You can see activity but cannot explain where opportunities stall.

WHAT A SCOPED PROJECT CAN INCLUDE

Concrete work.
A clear handoff.

01

Data review & cleanup

Check the agreed exports for duplicate records, missing fields, inconsistent categories, and totals that do not reconcile.

02

Metric definitions

Document what each measure includes, its source, its time period, and the limits of what it can tell you.

03

Reporting & visualization

Organize the findings in a spreadsheet, dashboard, or management report designed around the people who use it.

04

Interpretation & next steps

Explain the main observations, surface uncertainty, and recommend a practical next check rather than a premature conclusion.

The scope depends on data access, quality, volume, and the question. Live integrations, large-scale data engineering, predictive models, or regulated datasets are not assumed to be included.

THE BRICAL APPROACH

A finding is not always
an explanation.

A source can produce more inquiries but fewer bookings. That is a useful observation. It does not, on its own, prove the cause. Brical reports distinguish measured facts from hypotheses, and include the next information needed to evaluate them.

COMMON QUESTIONS

A few things worth
knowing upfront.

Can we begin with spreadsheets?

Yes. An agreed export or spreadsheet can be enough for an initial review. A new dashboard subscription is not automatically required.

Can you tell me exactly why sales changed?

Not from a chart alone. We can identify patterns and competing explanations, then determine which additional information or test would make the conclusion more reliable.

What information should I send initially?

Describe the question and the type of data available. Do not email passwords, sensitive personal records, or client datasets in the first inquiry. Access and handling should be agreed before any data transfer.
