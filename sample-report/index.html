What the records show.

Seventeen of 48 inquiries became booked jobs in this sample. Fourteen qualified leads are not marked booked. Their actual stage needs review.

What they do not show.

A booking is not a completed job or collected revenue. This dataset does not contain cancellations, margins, or marketing costs.

Volume is not the whole picture.

Fictional source-level results. Rates use all inquiries as the denominator.

An observation.

Paid search has more inquiries than referrals, but fewer booked jobs. That difference is worth investigating.

A limitation.

Small counts and a single period do not establish a reliable long-term ranking of channels. Lead quality, follow-up, and capacity may all matter.

Turn the report into a work plan.

01

Check the records.

Review the 14 qualified, unbooked leads. Confirm whether they are awaiting an estimate, lost, duplicated, or simply missing an update.

02

Review the handoff.

Compare the paid-search inquiries with service eligibility and follow-up records. Do not assume the channel itself is the problem.

03

Measure the next period.

Keep definitions consistent, record what changed, and collect costs and completed-job outcomes before making an ROI claim.
